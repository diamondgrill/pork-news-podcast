Monday, August 31st. Here is your pork industry news. Today's update centers on one story: fresh European Union pig herd figures. Let's get into it.

According to Eurostat data reported by Pig World, the European Union's pig herd stood at 131.5 million head at the end of 2025. That is a decline of point five percent compared with the previous year. Looking back further, the EU herd is now down 10 percent compared with 2020 levels. The picture varies widely by country. Spain's herd fell three percent to 33.6 million head. The Netherlands saw a sharper drop, down seven percent to 9.5 million head. Denmark moved in the opposite direction, with its herd rising six percent to 12.3 million head. Germany also grew, up one percent to 21.5 million head. On the breeding side, the trend turned positive. The EU's breeding sow herd rose point eight percent to 10.3 million head, a shift back to growth. Piglets under 20 kilograms increased six point one percent to 42.4 million head. That is the highest piglet count in the past decade. Denmark's live pig exports also hit a milestone, rising one percent to a record 18.5 million head. Taken together, the figures show the EU's overall pig population still edging lower. At the same time, the breeding herd and piglet numbers are turning upward. Individual member states, however, continue to move in different directions.

That's your pork industry news for today.
