Wednesday, September 2nd. Here is your pork industry news, your daily briefing on the global pork industry. Today we cover the latest hog and grain futures action and a fresh look at the shrinking North American breeding herd. We'll also cover trade developments touching Brazil and Argentina, plus retail trends out of the United Kingdom. As always, we'll take these stories one at a time, starting with the numbers out of the cash and futures markets.

Let's start with prices and futures markets in the United States. According to a market report from The Pig Site, citing USDA figures, the U.S. wholesale pork carcass price rose 1 dollar 36 cents on August 31st, reaching 95 dollars 16 cents per hundredweight. That was the previous day's move. The CME's lead live hog contract, October lean hogs, closed that same session up 1.775 cents at 83.675 cents per pound. Remember, the lean hog contract tracks live hogs, not cuts of pork meat, so this is a measure of hog values coming off the farm. Cattle markets were digesting some bearish news that day, including the reopening of the Mexico border and a temporary exemption on beef import tariffs. Even so, October live cattle futures edged up 0.95 cent to 212.675 cents, and October feeder cattle rose half a cent to 317.025 cents. Boxed beef told a different story. Choice boxed beef fell 1 dollar 3 cents to 375 dollars 20 cents, while Select dropped 6 dollars 57 cents to 354 dollars 51 cents.

Moving ahead to September 1st at the Chicago Board of Trade, and drawing on figures reported by Brownfield Ag News, the tone in livestock futures turned softer. October lean hog futures slipped 2 cents to close at 83.65 cents per pound. October live cattle fell 60 cents to 212.07 cents. October feeder cattle dropped 1 dollar 62 cents to 315.40 cents. So across the board, livestock contracts eased back that day. Feed costs told the opposite story, moving higher and adding pressure on the input side for hog producers. December corn climbed 8.25 cents to 5 dollars 46 cents a bushel. November soybeans jumped 29.75 cents to 13 dollars 18 cents. October soybean meal rose 7 dollars 10 cents to 345 dollars 70 cents. Crude oil also moved up, with the October contract gaining 4 dollars 46 cents to 90 dollars 22 cents.

That grain strength followed a session on August 31st, covered by Suinocultura Industrial, in which soybeans briefly touched a 26 month high before settling back. November soybeans finished that day roughly flat, at 12 dollars 88 cents a bushel. January soybeans edged up 0.04 percent to 13 dollars 3 cents. December corn rose 0.23 percent to 5 dollars 37 cents, marking a third straight weekly gain. That strength in corn came on concerns that the U.S. yield will come in below the August USDA estimate. Falling feed production in the European Union and reported Black Sea port damage in Ukraine added to the pressure. December wheat moved the other way, falling 1.28 percent to 7 dollars 74 cents. Funds were liquidating wheat positions, and Turkey helped broker progress on reopening Black Sea shipping routes, both weighing on that market.

Turning to supply, a column by Jim Long, carried by Suinocultura Industrial, examined the latest U.S. and Canadian breeding herd figures. It used U.S. data as of June 1st and Canadian data as of July 1st. The combined U.S. and Canadian breeding herd fell 54,000 head from a year earlier. Measured against 2021, the herd is down 523,000 head. Productivity has improved over that same span. Pigs weaned per sow per year rose from 21.06 in 2021 to 23.16 this year, a meaningful gain in efficiency. But the math on total hog numbers is less encouraging. Since 2021, the pig crop has grown by roughly 1.9 million head, while market hog marketings rose only about 530,000 head over that period. That gap of close to 1.4 million head is attributed to higher mortality between birth and market. In Canada, the picture looks somewhat different. The breeding herd there was roughly flat, while production actually rose 4 percent year over year. That gain was helped by 15,000 more sows in the Canadian herd and by reduced losses from PED. On top of that, the Canadian dollar trading near 72 U.S. cents is giving Canadian producers a relative cost advantage against their U.S. counterparts.

In trade news, JBS controlling shareholder Joesley Batista met with President Trump. He was lobbying in Washington for lower U.S. tariffs on Brazilian beef, according to a Wall Street Journal report cited by G1 Agro and carried by Suinocultura Industrial. The backdrop is that the U.S. cattle herd is at its lowest level in 75 years. That shortage has pushed U.S. meat prices higher. Easing tariffs on Brazilian beef could also open market access for other Brazilian protein exports, including pork. The U.S. administration has already rolled out some livestock support measures. These include loans for small meat processors and a preference for U.S. product in public procurement. However, those measures are seen as slow to take effect. On the broader trade picture, Brazil's exports across all products from January through August totaled 218.6 billion dollars on an FOB basis. That covered 490.1 million tons of goods, according to Secex and MDIC data.

In Argentina, according to Pig333, pork trade data for the first half of 2026 also stood out. June imports were up 39.8 percent year over year, reaching 6,133 tons. That nears the record of 6,220 tons that was set in April of 2025. Across the first half of the year as a whole, average monthly imports were 5,211 tons, up just 0.3 percent year over year. A 3.9 percent drop in unit prices meant the dollar value of those imports actually fell 3.6 percent. On the export side, the picture was stronger. May shipments reached 1,912 tons, a five year high. That topped the previous record of 1,860 tons from July of 2024 by 2.8 percent. Average monthly exports for the first half rose from 817 tons to 1,535 tons, an increase of 87.9 percent. As a result of that export growth, Argentina's pork trade deficit narrowed from 93.7 million dollars to 83.1 million dollars, a decline of 11.3 percent.

Let's turn to retail trends in the United Kingdom, in a report carried by Pig World. AHDB data from Worldpanel by Numerator covers the 12 weeks to August 9th. UK pork retail volumes fell 0.9 percent year over year during that period. Even so, prices rose 1.4 percent, which was enough to lift total spending 0.5 percent. Looking within the category, fresh pork volumes actually rose 0.8 percent. Pork mince was a standout, up 26.6 percent, as shoppers shifted away from beef mince toward pork. Pork belly also performed well, rising 8.8 percent. Processed pork moved the other way, falling 2.9 percent overall. Bacon rashers were down 1,673 tons, the biggest decline within that processed category. Added value products fared better, rising 6.6 percent in volume and 7.7 percent in spending. That growth was led by sous vide items, up 13 percent, and marinated products, up 8.9 percent.

In other developments, and also via Pig333, the Mato Grosso state legislature in Brazil passed the first reading of a bill on August 19th. The bill would allow pork to be added to public school lunch menus across the state. The measure, bill 1888 of 2025, was pushed by the state pork association Acrismat. It still needs to clear a second reading before heading to the governor for approval or veto. The association frames the bill as a step toward boosting the value and market reach of the state's pork industry.

That wraps up today's roundup of prices, supply, trade, and retail news. That's your pork industry news for today.
