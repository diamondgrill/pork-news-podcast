Thursday, August 20th. Here is your pork industry news. Today we cover CME lean hog prices and a mixed market outlook from Texas A and M. We also look at African swine fever spreading among wild boar in Finland. And we cover new trade developments touching Spain, New Zealand, and Brazil. Plus, a major cold storage investment from Indiana Packers, and a setback for Proposition 12 relief in the Senate farm bill.

Let's start with prices and market conditions. On the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, the October lean hog contract closed Tuesday, August 19th, up 0.77 cents to 81.50 cents per pound, according to Brownfield Ag News. Feed markets moved higher as well. September corn futures rose 9.75 cents to 4 dollars and 73 cents a bushel. September soybean meal gained 5 dollars and 90 cents to 318 dollars and 90 cents. Meanwhile, October live cattle fell 1 dollar and 70 cents to 217 dollars and 22 cents, and September feeder cattle dropped 4 dollars and 22 cents to 329 dollars and 12 cents.

The day before, Monday, August 18th, the October lean hog contract had slipped a fraction, down 0.01 cents to 80.725 cents per pound, reports The Pig Site. Seasonal demand softness and a consumer shift toward chicken and beef were cited as factors. USDA's pork carcass cutout value rose 37 cents to 99 dollars and 12 cents per hundredweight, with the belly primal up 1 dollar and 60 cents to 157 dollars and 30 cents. October live cattle edged up 0.15 cents to 218.925 cents, and packer margins swung to a profit of 53 dollars and 85 cents per head.

Texas A and M economist David Anderson offered a broader read on the market, as reported by Pork Business. Year to date, US pork production is up 1.3 percent, though the most recent four weeks show a decline of about 0.5 percent. The national hog price averaged about 97 cents per pound last week, down from roughly 1 dollar and 7 cents a year ago. Even so, farrow to finish operations have now posted profits for 25 consecutive months, thanks largely to cheap feed. This year's per-head profits have ranged from about 2 dollars and 60 cents to 52 dollars. July's consumer price index showed retail pork at 4 dollars and 89 cents per pound, down from 5 dollars and 1 cent a year earlier. Beef rose from 9 dollars and 69 cents to 10 dollars and 49 cents. Barrow and gilt carcass weights averaged 211 pounds last week, above last year's 209 pounds.

Globally, the FAO Meat Price Index averaged 127.7 points in July, falling 3.6 points, or 2.8 percent, from June for its first month-over-month decline of 2026. The index remains 0.8 percent above year-ago levels. Pork prices fell on ample EU supply and weak global demand, though summer heat slowing weight gains provided some support. Lamb was the only meat category to rise, hitting a record high.

Turning to animal health, African swine fever continues to spread among wild boar in Finland. Suinocultura Industrial reports the number of infected wild boar has risen to 19. They are concentrated near Virolahti, within 3 kilometers of the Russian border, across two sites about 5 kilometers apart. Finland's food agency, Ruokavirasto, has added the town of Miehikala to the core restriction zone. The buffer zone now spans parts of Virolahti, Miehikala, and Lappeenranta. About 400 volunteer hunters have been mobilized to search for carcasses. No infections have been confirmed on commercial pig farms as of August 18th.

In the United States, a group of senators has asked Secretary of State Rubio to restore funding for New World screwworm control, National Hog Farmer reports. The dissolution of USAID cut 250 million dollars earmarked for the FAO's transboundary animal disease programs. It also cut 72 million dollars for 16 Feed the Future Innovation Labs.

On trade, Spain's Serrano ham exports to the United States grew sharply in the first five months of 2026. Pig333 reports that volume rose 39.65 percent to 1,534 tons, up from about 1,099 tons, while value climbed 20.61 percent to 19.16 million euros. Average price, however, fell 13.64 percent to 12.49 euros per kilogram. In 2025, certified sales from the Serrano Ham Consortium reached 593,301 kilograms, up 13.65 percent, with the US remaining its largest market outside Spain. The consortium flagged potential US tariffs as a concern.

In New Zealand, more than 60 percent of pork consumed is imported, yet awareness of US pork remains low among both industry and consumers, according to Euromeat News. US pork exports to New Zealand have averaged 46 million dollars annually over the past two years, mostly used in bacon and ham processing. New Zealand restricts fresh pork imports outside processing to packages under 3 kilograms, and Australia does not allow the sale of US fresh pork at all. USMEF held a quality masterclass and consumer tasting event in Auckland, drawing about 300 attendees.

In Brazil, the state of Goias saw pork exports rise 15.3 percent in the first half of the year to 10,800 tons, worth 20 million dollars, Suinocultura Industrial reports. Singapore was the top destination, accounting for 47.5 percent of value at 9.5 million dollars, followed by Hong Kong, Angola, Vietnam, and Georgia. Nationally, Brazil's pork exports for January through June rose 10.5 percent to 773,100 tons, worth 1.8 billion dollars, an 8.3 percent increase. First quarter slaughter climbed 5.5 percent to 15.27 million head, the highest for that period on record.

Also from Brazil, tightening European Union restrictions on antimicrobial use in animal production are pushing up costs for Brazilian pork destined for Europe. Suinocultura Industrial reports the industry estimates production costs will rise by about 100 reais per head. The increase comes as processors build separated production lines and adopt restrictive protocols, audits, and added controls. According to the animal health trade group Sindan, antimicrobials fell from 17 percent of industry revenue in 2015 to 12 percent in 2025. Vertically integrated poultry and pork chains are said to be ahead of the more fragmented beef chain in adapting.

Turning to packer news, Indiana Packers has opened a 44 million dollar frozen distribution center in Delphi, Indiana, National Hog Farmer reports. The facility more than doubles the company's warehouse space and adds 100,000 square feet of frozen storage capacity.

In the UK, animal nutrition company Harbro has acquired Cargill's animal nutrition site in Dalton, North Yorkshire, which produces pig feed, according to Pig World. The deal includes the feed mill and related operations, with integration expected to be complete in early 2027. The site will continue operating as a separate company. The acquisition follows ABN's announcement weeks earlier that it would exit the pig and poultry sectors, as consolidation continues across the UK feed industry.

On the policy front, a fix for California's Proposition 12 has dropped out of the stalled Senate farm bill. It had remained in the House-passed version, Brownfield Ag News reports. Proposition 12 requires at least 24 square feet of space per gestating sow, well above the industry's science-based standard of about 15 square feet. Katie Brown, president of the Illinois Pork Producers Association, said compliance forces producers to either shrink their sow herds or fund costly barn expansions. She noted California retail prices are already running about 20 percent higher as a result. The Senate Agriculture Committee is expected to revisit the bill after its August recess.

In Brazil, a new law numbered 15,490 expands the government's Conab over-the-counter feed sales program beyond corn. It now also includes sorghum, cottonseed, soybean meal, and corn meal, Suinocultura Industrial reports. The existing monthly cap of 27 tons for small producers stays in place, while a new 80 ton monthly quota has been created for family farming cooperatives and associations. The previous annual purchase cap of 200,000 tons has been eliminated. Public auctions under the minimum price guarantee policy can now buy at up to 25 percent above the minimum price.

Also in Brazil, a University of Sao Paulo study estimates that zero or reduced tax rates on meat and dairy starting in 2027 would cut prices by more than 13 percent. The study appeared in the Journal of Environmental Economics and Management. The same policy would raise food-related emissions by 8.22 percent. The researchers project a net increase of 10.2 million tons of CO2 equivalent from Brazilian household consumption. That is about 1.71 percent of consumption-related emissions and 0.5 percent of the country's total inventory. They propose a 15.13 percent selective tax on carbon-intensive products, paired with a full cash rebate for low-income households.

Rounding out today's news with production data, the UK's Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs has new figures. July clean pig slaughter rose 5.8 percent year over year to 909,000 head. Pork production was up 5 percent to 83,000 tons, according to Pig World. Sow and boar slaughter climbed 12 percent, from 17,200 to 19,200 head, suggesting a shrinking breeding herd. Average carcass weight fell to 88.5 kilograms, down from 90 kilograms in June and 93.6 kilograms in April, confirming that last year's processing backlog has cleared. For the January through July period, clean pig slaughter is up 3 percent and sow and boar slaughter up 8 percent. Scotland stood out with a 44 percent increase, from 9,900 to 14,300 head.

In Belgium, The Pig Site reports that Flanders had an average pig density of 3.6 head per hectare in 2025, eleven times the EU27 average of 0.32. That compares with just 0.2 in Wallonia and a Belgian national average of 1.71. West Flanders province had the highest concentration within the region, with Meetjesland, Waasland, Noorderkempen, and northern Limburg also showing dense clustering. Among EU countries, Denmark had the highest density at 2.86 head per hectare, while Finland had the lowest at 0.03.

And in Brazil, Suinocultura Industrial reports on Master Agroindustrial, a 32 year old company based in Videira, Santa Catarina. It is building a genetic nucleus farm for 5,000 sows in the town of Canoinhas. The project is a cornerstone of the company's strategy to double the domestic and international presence of its Sulita brand. It is meant to support ongoing improvements in production and health metrics across its vertically integrated operations.

That's your pork industry news for today.
