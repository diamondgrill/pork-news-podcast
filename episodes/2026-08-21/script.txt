Friday, August 21st. Here is your pork industry news. Today we cover CME lean hog futures and a mixed picture for US pork production. We also look at where African swine fever stands in Spain and Taiwan, and at heavy financial losses hitting China's largest hog producer. And we cover a deepening price crisis in Brazil, a wave of Brazilian production and export developments, and a new import warning out of Japan.

Let's start with prices and markets. On the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, the October lean hog contract, which tracks live hog values, closed Wednesday, August 19th, up 0.775 cents to 81.5 cents per pound. That's according to The Pig Site. USDA's pork carcass cutout value moved the other direction that day. It fell 0.76 dollars to 98.36 dollars per hundredweight. In cattle, Tyson Foods announced last week it would close and sell three beef plants. Weak consumer confidence is weighing on livestock futures broadly, including hogs.

The next day, Thursday, August 20th, the October lean hog contract gave back ground. It fell 1.27 dollars to 80.22 dollars per hundredweight, Brownfield Ag News reports. On the feed side, September corn futures moved higher, rising 5 and three quarter cents to 4 dollars and 78 and three quarter cents a bushel. September soybean meal moved the other way, falling 2 dollars and 40 cents to 315 dollars and 70 cents.

Zooming out, US hog prices have surged recently. Even so, they remain below year-ago levels, according to Feedstuffs. Producer profitability right now is being supported mainly by one factor: lower feed costs.

On the production side, heavier hog weights are the key factor sustaining US pork production volume in 2026, National Hog Farmer reports. At the same time, the export outlook has been revised down. That's due to competition from Canada and Brazil, along with uncertain demand from Asian trading partners.

USDA figures released this week show total US red meat production in July fell 2 percent year over year, to 4.287 billion pounds. Beef production dropped 5 percent to 2.085 billion pounds. Pork moved the opposite direction, rising 1 percent to 2.191 billion pounds, Brownfield Ag News reports. Hog slaughter numbers dipped slightly, to 10.264 million head. But average live weight rose 4 pounds, to 286 pounds, and that increase pushed overall production higher.

Overseas, China's hog sector is under real financial strain. Top producer Muyuan Foods posted a net loss of 6.08 billion yuan in the first half of 2026 amid weak pork prices in the domestic Chinese market. Operating cash flow also turned negative during the same period, underscoring how deep the price slump has cut into producer margins at even the largest scale operations.

Brazil is seeing a similar squeeze. Cepea data show the average live hog price in the SP-5 region fell to 5.18 reais per kilogram in July. In real terms, that is below even the worst point of the 2022 crisis, when the deflated price hit 5.97 reais per kilogram back in February of that year. Suinocultura Industrial attributes the slump to two forces at once. First, domestic oversupply paired with weak consumption at home. Second, a slowdown in June and July exports to Asian buyers such as the Philippines, which had built up inventory earlier in the year.

Turning to animal health, Catalonia in Spain has confirmed no new African swine fever cases since its last update on August 13th, Pig333 reports. The cumulative total remains steady at 67 cases, three primary and 64 secondary, with 379 positive wild boar spread across 13 municipalities. Surveillance continues at scale. So far 10,952 wild boar have tested negative, and boar culling within the infected zone has reached 10,727 animals.

Meanwhile in the United States, the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service has lifted import restrictions on Taiwan. The move was based on Taiwan's African swine fever status, National Hog Farmer reports. That said, the change is limited. USDA still does not consider Taiwan free of foot and mouth disease, classical swine fever, or vesicular disease of swine.

On trade and country markets, Canada's agriculture minister traveled to the Iowa State Fair this week. There, the minister reinforced the importance of the trade partnership with the United States, National Hog Farmer reports. The numbers back up that message. Two way agriculture, food, and seafood trade between Canada and Iowa totaled 3 billion Canadian dollars in 2025.

In Italy, first half 2026 pig slaughter totaled 5,007,997 head, down 0.9 percent year over year, Pig333 reports. Two regions dominate the national count. Emilia Romagna accounted for 1.83 million head, or 36.6 percent of the total, and Lombardy for 1.66 million head, or 33.1 percent. Together they made up nearly 70 percent of Italy's slaughter. Third place Piedmont stood out for growth, rising 10.6 percent to 424,542 head.

In Japan, the agriculture ministry's Animal Quarantine Service issued a warning against illegally bringing in and selling meat and meat products. It was distributed through the Japan Pork Producers Association. Since July 1st, 2026, domestic sales of meat products that have not cleared import quarantine are newly prohibited. The penalties are significant. Violations can bring fines of up to 3 million yen for individuals, up to 50 million yen for corporations, and imprisonment of up to 3 years.

Turning to feed costs, Brazil's domestic corn consumption is set to top 100 million tons for the first time ever in the 2026-27 season. That would be a historic milestone for the country's feed and ethanol sectors combined. The figure comes from Hedgepoint estimates cited by Suinocultura Industrial. Much of that growth is coming from ethanol. Ethanol-related corn use has grown from 6.5 million tons back in 2000 to 28.5 million tons now. On the supply side, production is projected at 140.3 million tons, roughly flat with last year. The export forecast, however, was revised down to 41 million tons amid competition from the United States and Argentina.

Rounding out today's news with a wave of Brazilian production and export developments. Brazilian cooperative Coopavel is investing more than 100 million reais in its piglet production unit in Cascavel, Parana state, Suinocultura Industrial reports. The expansion plan is substantial. It grows the sow herd from 12,000 to 20,000 head by mid-2027 and raises daily slaughter capacity from 1,800 to 3,000 head, or 720,000 head a year. The cooperative is also launching what it calls Project 35 in September, aiming for 35 weaned piglets per sow annually.

Brazilian government statistics agency IBGE reports second quarter 2026 hog slaughter of 15.58 million head. That's up 3.2 percent year over year and 2 percent from the prior quarter. Carcass production followed the same trend, reaching 1.49 million tons, up 4.8 percent year over year and 4.5 percent quarter over quarter.

Santa Catarina state posted record meat exports from January through July. The total reached 1.24 million tons, up 9 percent year over year, worth 2.84 billion dollars, itself a 12.6 percent increase, Suinocultura Industrial reports. Pork made up a large share of that figure. It accounted for 437,000 tons and 1.09 billion dollars of the total. That means the state alone was responsible for 51.3 percent of Brazil's entire pork export value and 48.5 percent of its export volume.

Also in Brazil, health regulator Anvisa took action on a food safety issue. It suspended sale, distribution, and consumption of a Suinco pork loin product called Lombo Canadense, sold in one kilogram packages. The reason was straightforward: nitrite and nitrate levels exceeded allowed limits. The affected lot, number 070726, was produced July 7th and carries an October 5th expiration date. The action follows a voluntary recall the manufacturer had already begun on its own, before regulators stepped in.

That wraps up today's roundup of prices, disease status, trade, and production news from across the pork industry. That's your pork industry news for today.
