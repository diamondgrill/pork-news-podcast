Saturday, September 5th. Here is your pork industry news.

Today we cover a soft week for U.S. hog and pork prices, more African swine fever activity in Spain with a first approved vaccine in the Philippines, and shifting pork trade flows in the UK, Spain, and Brazil. We will also look at tightening feed grain supplies across Europe, two packer stories, and country updates from Germany, Brazil, and the UK.

Let's start with prices and futures. On September 2nd, CME October lean hog futures settled at 83.775 cents per pound, up slightly from the day before. The USDA pork carcass cutout that day was 96 dollars and 11 cents per hundredweight, down about 1 dollar and 79 cents. October live cattle futures fell to 210.175 cents per pound, while choice boxed beef rose to 382 dollars and 5 cents per hundredweight. On September 3rd, October lean hog futures eased further to 83.45 cents per pound. The pork cutout that day dropped sharply, down 2 dollars and 77 cents to 92 dollars and 85 cents per hundredweight. Corn futures for December slipped to 5 dollars and 40 and three-quarter cents a bushel, while November soybeans rose to 13 dollars and 16 and a quarter cents. Cattle futures bounced that day after President Trump signaled a temporary easing of beef tariffs. Heading into the weekend on September 4th, October lean hog futures fell again, settling at 82.30 dollars. That was down 1 dollar and 15 cents, and December fell 1 dollar and 45 cents to 72.47. The national five-day average for negotiated cash hogs was 90 dollars and 15 cents. The pork cutout firmed slightly to 92 dollars and 86 cents, up 1 dollar and 74 cents, as belly prices jumped more than 12 dollars. Estimated hog slaughter came in at 424,000 head, down 23,000 from the previous week. That was also down more than 48,000 from a year earlier.

Turning to disease and biosecurity. In Spain's Catalonia region, five new African swine fever cases in wild boar were confirmed after two weeks with no new detections. The cases were found in Barcelona, Molins de Rei, and Sant Cugat del Valles. That brings the regional total to 384 cases across 13 municipalities. Catalonia's government approved a support package on September 1st for slaughterhouses, farms, and businesses hit by the outbreak's movement restrictions. The aid covers administrative and health costs tied to exports and slaughter. It also includes subsidized loans for farms that struggled to sell during the first three months of restrictions. Businesses in high-risk municipalities whose 2026 sales fell more than 30 percent can apply for grants. Applications are expected to open in February of 2027. In the Philippines, regulators approved commercial sale of a live attenuated African swine fever vaccine made by Vietnam's AVAC. It is approved for fattening pigs and is the country's first approved vaccine against the disease. Since African swine fever arrived in the Philippines in 2019, the national hog herd has shrunk from about 13 million head to about 8 million. Outbreaks have now been recorded in 76 of the country's 82 provinces. A second vaccine from South Korea is still in trials and could become available next year. Separately, Pig Progress reported a new African swine fever case in wild boar in Finland. It was found about 40 kilometers west of the country's existing outbreak area. And in the United States, Iowa State's Gustavo Silva says PRRS case rates have been running higher than usual lately. He attributes this mainly to growing pig herds, while breeding herd cases remain comparatively low. He notes that devastating new PRRS variants used to emerge roughly once a decade but now appear every two to three years. Some strains can survive up to 24 hours at 97 degrees Fahrenheit and as long as seven days in winter conditions. Air filtration cuts outbreak risk on breeding farms by about 50 percent compared with unfiltered farms. Closing a herd within two weeks of an outbreak is the most effective step toward stabilization.

Now to trade and tariffs. In the UK, AHDB's analysis of trade data shows pork exports rose 11 percent year over year in the first half of 2026, to 173,200 tonnes, worth 267.2 million pounds. Second quarter shipments were 84,700 tonnes, down 4 percent from the first quarter. Shipments to China totaled 34,200 tonnes, down 3 percent from a year earlier. Shipments to the EU rose 19 percent to 73,100 tonnes for the half year. UK pork imports fell 5.6 percent to roughly 350,000 tonnes, pushing self-sufficiency up from 61 percent to 66 percent. In Spain, the meat industry group ANICE says first-half pork and processed pork exports to Japan and Mexico together fell by about 350 million euros. Shipments to Japan totaled 24,880 tonnes from January through June, down 73.9 percent from 95,425 tonnes a year earlier. Their value fell 77.5 percent to 90.6 million euros. Exports to Mexico fell to zero, compared with 5,154 tonnes worth 9.8 million euros in the same period last year. On the regulatory front, the European Commission's suspension of Brazilian animal product imports took effect September 3rd, over concerns about antimicrobial-use compliance. The suspension covers beef, poultry, horsemeat, farmed seafood, eggs, honey, and casings, but pork is not included. Brazil's government said the same day it could take reciprocal measures if talks do not produce a satisfactory outcome. Brazil's own pork exports kept growing despite that dispute. According to the Brazilian Animal Protein Association, July pork exports rose 3.7 percent year over year to 131,500 tonnes. Revenue for the month fell 5.6 percent to 298.3 million dollars. For the year through July, cumulative exports reached 925,600 tonnes, up 9.1 percent, worth 2.158 billion dollars. The Philippines was the top destination in July at 20,300 tonnes, followed by Japan at 18,400 tonnes, Chile at 13,900 tonnes, and China at 9,100 tonnes. Meanwhile, the Canadian Meat Council says Canadian pork exports to Mexico rose nearly 20 percent in 2025. That was more than 300,000 tonnes, worth over 371 million dollars, a record. China's 25 percent tariff has hurt competitiveness of higher-value Canadian cuts, though demand for offal remains solid. More than 80 North American agri-food groups are backing renewal of the USMCA trade agreement.

Feed costs and grain supply are also in focus. The FAO food price index climbed to 133.3 in August, up from a revised 130.8 in July and the highest level since November 2022. The cereal component rose 2.2 percent for the month, vegetable oils rose 0.6 percent, and sugar jumped 11.9 percent. FAO also cut its 2026 world cereal production forecast by 3.4 million tonnes, to 2.98 billion tonnes. That is a 2 percent annual decline that would be the steepest since 2018. Europe's feed industry federation, FEFAC, says repeated heatwaves are straining the EU feed supply chain. New-season corn import needs are up by more than 7 million tonnes, with total imports expected above 26 million tonnes. Those imports are expected mainly from Ukraine, the United States, and Brazil. FEFAC estimates additional soybean meal sourcing costs above 1 billion euros in 2027. It is urging the EU to reactivate crisis measures last used in 2022. Separately, the European Commission's late-August estimate puts the 2026 EU corn harvest at 50.1 million tonnes. That is roughly a 20-year low, about 20 percent below the recent three-year average. Hungary's harvest is expected around 2.5 million tonnes, flipping the country from exporter to importer. Romania's harvest of roughly 8 million tonnes is down more than half from 2018 and 2019 levels. And in France, the government announced more than 1 billion euros, or about 1.16 billion dollars, in aid on September 4th. The aid is for farmers hit by record heat and drought, split between rapid payments and a recovery fund for seed and feed purchases. French livestock and crop sectors each estimate losses above 5 billion euros. Some 30,000 to 35,000 farms are described as facing financial difficulty.

On the packer and acquisition front, Spain's Grupo Costa has acquired Paraguayan pork assets from Grupo Concepcion for an estimated 200 million dollars. The deal includes the Incka Foods meat plant and breeding and finishing farms. It is part of a broader plan by Costa Foods to invest more than 300 million dollars in Paraguay to build export-oriented pork production. In the United States, Tyson Foods cut its fiscal 2026 adjusted operating profit outlook to between 1.85 billion and 2.05 billion dollars. That is down from its prior range of 2.1 to 2.3 billion dollars. The company also lowered its annual sales growth forecast, citing pressure on its beef segment from expected lower cattle prices. It marks Tyson's second downward revision within a month.

A few country market notes to close out the main news. In Germany, final government data show about 14,550 hog farms as of May, down 4.6 percent from a year earlier and down 41 percent from 2016. Total hog inventory held roughly steady at about 20.95 million head. In Brazil, the research group Cepea says average live hog prices in Sao Paulo's SP-5 region fell to 4.97 reais per kilogram in August. That is a record low in data going back to 2002, as strong production continued to outpace processor demand. And in the UK, AHDB analysis found that 18.3 million people bought outdoor-bred pork in the year to mid-June. That accounts for about 12 percent of total pork sales volume, at an average price well above conventional pork.

A handful of other developments. Canada's government is putting 3.1 million dollars into hog disease surveillance, funding testing that can detect multiple swine viruses from a single sample. China is working to raise soybean and corn yields on its existing farmland rather than expanding acreage, since it still imports about 80 percent of its soybeans from Brazil. The European Parliament's agriculture committee voted to roll back proposed industrial emissions rules for livestock farms to earlier, less restrictive thresholds. U.S. pork exports in July were held back by ongoing pseudorabies concerns, though shipments to Mexico improved somewhat from the prior month. And in Mexico, producer Altosano Granjas Carroll completed the country's first pork export shipment to Paraguay, according to local trade media.

That's your pork industry news for today.
