Tuesday, August 25th. Here is your pork industry news.

Coming up today, hog futures and cash markets moved on two separate USDA price reports out of the United States. Those reports covered both the lean hog futures market and the pork carcass cutout. Feed costs are under pressure as well, as Brazil's soybean market hits a multi year high and China accelerates its buying of new crop supplies for the coming marketing year. Spain reported a milestone in its fight against African swine fever, with a full week free of new cases in one high risk zone. And we'll round out the show with a packer update from southern Brazil, plus a handful of trade headlines from Korea, Spain, and Mexico.

Let's start with prices and the futures market. Brownfield Ag News reported that the October lean hog contract closed on August 24th at 81.12 cents per pound. That was up a quarter cent from the previous session. In that same session, the grain and feed complex was mixed. September corn rose 7.75 cents to settle at 4 dollars 91 and a half cents a bushel. September soybean meal rose 2 dollars 60 cents to 320 dollars 30 cents a ton. September soybeans slipped 9 cents to 12 dollars 16 cents a bushel. Cattle futures moved the other way that day. October live cattle fell sharply, down 4 dollars 32 cents to 213 dollars 60 cents per hundredweight.

The Pig Site also covered the cash market, citing a separate USDA report. USDA put Friday's pork carcass wholesale price at 97 dollars 96 cents per hundredweight. That was up 1 dollar 47 cents from the day before. The most actively traded October lean hog contract that same day rose 0.65 cents, to 80.875 cents per pound. Cattle markets were softer on that Friday session. They were weighed down by news that President Trump had announced a 90 day measure expanding a low tariff quota for ground beef, to 300,000 metric tons. October live cattle eased 0.075 cents that day, closing at 217.925 cents.

Looking further out, National Hog Farmer highlighted USDA's longer range price outlook for the hog market. The agency is forecasting the average live hog price at 65 dollars 32 cents per hundredweight for 2026. For 2027, USDA's forecast comes down slightly, to 65 dollars per hundredweight. National Hog Farmer said that outlook suggests the price peak for this cycle has likely already passed.

Turning now to feed costs, Suinocultura Industrial reported that Brazil's soybean index has climbed to its highest nominal level since April of 2023. The Cepea and ESALQ index, which covers the Paranaguá port and Paraná state, has been pushed higher by strong competition for physical supply. Chinese exporters and domestic Brazilian crushers are both bidding for the same beans. Concerns about an El Niño weather pattern have also made producers reluctant to sell right now. That reluctance to sell is tightening the cash market even further. Cepea also cited USDA figures on the wider global supply picture. The world soybean ending stocks to use ratio is seen falling to 33.5 percent in the 2025 to 2026 season. That would be the lowest level since the 2022 to 2023 season. A further drop, to 32.3 percent, is forecast for the following season, 2026 to 2027.

Staying with feed, Canal Rural reported that Brazil's compound feed production rose 5.4 percent year over year in the first quarter of 2026. Full year output there is expected to reach 94.9 million tonnes. That increase is being driven mainly by demand from the poultry and pork production chains, according to the industry group cited in the report.

The Pig Site also reported on the demand side of the soybean market. China has accelerated its purchases of new crop US soybeans since late June, with buyers there building coverage ahead for the 2026 to 2027 marketing year. On July 8th alone, China booked 472,000 tonnes of new crop supply. Of that total, 336,000 tonnes was specifically earmarked for the 2026 to 2027 crop year. In the week ending July 23rd, total new crop sales reached about 1.33 million tonnes. China accounted for roughly 519,000 tonnes of that figure, or 39 percent of the week's total. Actual shipments, though, remain seasonally slow at this point in the year. They were running at a seven day average of about 11,000 tonnes a day as of late July. In other words, sales bookings continue to run well ahead of the actual pace of shipments out of US ports.

Now to disease and biosecurity, where Pig World reported some encouraging news out of Spain. The Catalonia region recorded zero new African swine fever positives in its high risk zone over the past week. That is the first time this has happened since the virus first entered the region back in November of 2025. Authorities there tested 302 wild boar samples, and every single one came back negative for the virus. In that same week, 333 boars were removed from the infected zone as part of ongoing control efforts. That brings the cumulative cull since the operation began to 10,727 animals in total. Regional officials said the result strengthens their confidence in the containment strategy that has been in place. Even so, access restrictions covering 19 municipalities remain in effect for now, and officials say those control measures will continue for the time being.

National Hog Farmer also reported on a federal watchdog finding closer to home in the United States. USDA's Office of Inspector General reviewed the National Bio and Agro-Defense Facility, known by the acronym NBAF. Investigators found that it has not been properly configured or commissioned by the Department of Homeland Security. That matters because the facility is meant to support high level biosafety research and livestock disease diagnostics for the country. Senator Moran responded to the watchdog's report on August 24th.

On the packer side, Suinocultura Industrial reported that talks are advancing over a hog slaughter plant in Rio Grande do Sul, in southern Brazil. The Poço das Antas plant has sat idle since June of 2023. Another cooperative based in the state is now negotiating to buy the facility and bring it back online. A restart there is being targeted for sometime in 2026. The plant has capacity to process 1,700 hogs a day when running at full operation. Before it closed, it served roughly 200 integrated producers in the surrounding region. According to the report, the purchase price itself is largely agreed between the two sides already. The main issue still to be resolved involves the former operator, a cooperative called Languiru, and its outstanding debts. Specifically, the parties still need to work out how the collateral owed to creditors will be structured.

Finally, a few other developments worth noting briefly before we wrap up today. ALIC reported that a severe heat wave has significantly affected hog farming conditions in Spain this summer. As a result, South Korea's pork imports from Spain are said to be surging, though specific import volumes were not available in that report. Separately, the Spanish outlets Bolsamania and Diario Córdoba both reported that pork and olive oil were the top categories in Spain's 2025 agri-food exports. Detailed export figures for those categories were not confirmed in either report. And in Mexico, El Sol de México reported that live hog prices in the San Salvador district fell by 20 pesos per kilogram. That decline is squeezing producer margins in the area, though the resulting price level and the exact time frame of the drop were not specified.

That covers the futures and cash markets, feed costs, disease news, and packer and trade developments shaping the pork industry today. Today's reports came from the United States, Brazil, Spain, South Korea, and Mexico. That's your pork industry news for today.
