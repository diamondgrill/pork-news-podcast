Saturday, August 15th. Here is your pork industry news. Today we're covering a week of sliding lean hog futures alongside soft cash and cutout values in the US.
We'll also look at African swine fever developments in Spain and South Africa. Then there's a new US tariff on Chilean pork, and a Brazilian court ruling that winds down the country's soy moratorium.
After that we'll turn to packer earnings, feed cost pressures, and country-level production data from Europe, Mexico, and Japan. Let's start with prices and futures.

On August 12th, CME lean hog futures were mixed. The August contract fell 0.2 cents to 95.675 cents per pound, while the October contract rose 0.225 cents to 83.55 cents per pound.
Pork carcass cutout fell 1 dollar and 33 cents to 100 dollars and 15 cents per hundredweight. That same day's July consumer price index showed retail pork prices down 1.5 percent, the largest drop since November 2023.
On August 13th, Brownfield Ag News reported the CME October contract down 1.42 cents to 82.12 cents per pound on technical selling. The December contract fell 1.60 cents to 73.10 cents per pound.
Cash hogs told a different story that day. The National Daily Direct weighted average rose 1 dollar and 56 cents to 97 dollars and 37 cents.
Estimated slaughter reached 484,000 head, up 2,000 from the prior week and about 6,000 above a year ago. USDA's August 13th cutout figure fell 1 dollar and 23 cents to 98 dollars and 92 cents per hundredweight.
That marked a third straight decline and the lowest level since July 9th. The CME October contract closed that session down 1.425 cents at 82.125 cents per pound.
Heading into the weekend on August 14th, October slipped further, down 0.37 cents to 81.75 cents per pound. December eased 0.62 cents to 72.47 cents per pound.
Cash hogs fell sharply too, down 4 dollars and 7 cents on very light negotiated volume, with the base price range at 90 to 94.50 dollars.
Packers are securing the hogs they need without bidding aggressively, leaving demand into next week uncertain. A reminder: the CME lean hog contract tracks live hog values, not pork meat prices directly.

Turning to trade and tariffs, the US Trade Representative's office will impose an additional 12.5 percent tariff on Chilean pork and poultry. The decision follows a months-long forced-labor investigation.
The Chilean pork association ChileCarne sought an exemption through hearing testimony and written submissions, but the request was denied. The group says it will keep pushing for a reversal alongside the government and export associations.
Mexico's pork exports in the first half of 2026 totaled 92,684 tons worth 426 million dollars, up 12.9 percent in volume and 10 percent in value. Japan was the top destination, taking 67.8 percent of shipments and growing 15.4 percent.
Mexican pork imports came in at 919,289 tons worth 2 billion dollars, down 0.6 percent in volume and 16 percent in value, as the average import price fell 15.4 percent. Imports from Spain nearly disappeared, down 99.1 percent, due to that country's African swine fever outbreak.

On the disease front, week 33 African swine fever data out of Spain show one new site confirmed in Catalonia. It involved four wild boar, including two found within Barcelona city limits.
That brings the cumulative total to 67 sites and 379 positive boar across 13 municipalities. All other 10,650 boar tested elsewhere came back negative, and 306 wild boar were culled within the infection zone in the past week alone.
In South Africa, the country's largest-ever African swine fever outbreak, on commercial farms north of Pretoria, has been declared over. Full recovery on affected farms is expected to take 12 to 18 months.
Wholesale pork prices rose from about 32 rand per kilogram to about 40 rand per kilogram during the outbreak. They have since fallen to about 30 rand per kilogram as rising imports and recovering production created an oversupply.
Prices are expected to settle back near the long-term average of roughly 32 rand per kilogram. In the UK, the Food Standards Agency's National Food Crime Unit has seized 33 tons of illegally traded food since its operation began, and made one arrest.
That included mislabeled frozen products with no traceability. In June alone, 25 tons were destroyed, and local authorities carried out 111 inspections that recovered more than 8 tons.
Industry groups say the illegal meat trade raises concerns about introducing livestock disease.

Now to packer developments. MBRF, the company formed by Marfrig's acquisition of BRF, reported second-quarter net profit down 19.5 percent year over year to 69 million reais, about 13.3 million dollars.
Adjusted EBITDA rose 5.4 percent to 3.2 billion reais, net revenue increased 4.9 percent to 40.72 billion reais, and sales volume rose 1.5 percent to 1.97 million tons. High domestic interest rates and a strong Brazilian real weighed on results.
Canada's Maple Leaf Foods reported second-quarter adjusted EBITDA up 4.8 percent year over year to 137 million Canadian dollars, with margin expanding to 13.4 percent. Revenue rose 1.6 percent but missed market expectations.
Poultry segment sales grew 7.1 percent, while prepared foods sales fell 2 percent. The company maintained its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of 520 million to 540 million Canadian dollars.
JBS USA ended beef processing at its Souderton, Pennsylvania plant on August 14th. It will invest more than 30 million dollars over the next decade to convert the site into a case-ready, value-added facility.
The move avoids a closure and preserves roughly 400 jobs.

Turning to feed costs, Chicago grain markets jumped on August 12th, with December corn futures up 4.40 percent to 4 dollars and 80 cents per bushel.
The move followed USDA cutting its 2025-26 US corn ending stocks estimate to 49.40 million tons from 51.31 million tons. The agency also raised the export forecast to a record 86.36 million tons.
September wheat rose 3.32 percent to 6 dollars and 69 cents, and November soybeans gained 1.24 percent to 11 dollars and 83 cents, adding to feed cost pressure.
Ukraine's agriculture ministry said on August 14th that attacks on Black Sea ports could cut 2026-27 grain exports to about 29.6 million tons. That would be far short of a planned 64.4 million tons.
The ministry asked the EU for 220 million euros in support, projecting an 11-million-ton storage shortfall by November and unsold inventory worth more than 10.8 billion euros. The aid is meant to unlock 4 billion euros in bank loans through interest-rate subsidies.
In Brazil, the Supreme Federal Court on August 14th upheld the constitutionality of the soy moratorium and related state laws. Industry group Abiove said there are no plans to restart the moratorium framework.
In a related ruling, the court also upheld a state law revoking tax incentives for companies still participating in the moratorium. That effectively winds down the 20-year-old framework, since major traders including ADM, Cargill, and China's COFCO had already exited it in January.
Researchers estimate the wind-down could bring an additional 1.4 million hectares of deforestation and roughly 745 million tons of greenhouse gas emissions over the next decade. That carries implications for soy sourcing in hog feed supply chains.
Separately, Brazil's food supply agency Conab raised its 2025-26 grain production forecast 2.4 percent to 360.75 million tons.

In country markets, the European Commission's updated outlook projects 2026 EU net pork production up 0.3 percent to 22.1 million tons. That's supported by a 0.6 percent rise in the sow herd.
That would mark a stabilizing after two years of overproduction. Exports are forecast to fall 0.5 percent to 2.97 million tons on African swine fever in Spain, Chinese anti-dumping tariffs, and competition from Brazil in Asian markets.
Imports are seen falling 3 percent to 94,900 tons, while per capita consumption holds steady at 33.1 kilograms. In France, 2025 apparent pork consumption rose 2.7 percent to 2.172 million tons on a carcass-weight basis, or 31.5 kilograms per person.
Household purchases increased 1.7 percent as average prices fell 1.4 percent, with improved affordability driving the recovery. Imports rose 3.9 percent, pushing the import share of domestic consumption up to 29 percent.
Spain's pork production for the first half of 2026 reached 2.76 million tons on a carcass-weight basis, up 3.2 percent year over year. Output kept expanding even amid the country's African swine fever outbreak.
In Japan, an August survey from the agriculture ministry showed average retail prices for pork and chicken hitting record highs, with pork loin reaching 290 yen per 100 grams.
Reports point to a weaker yen, higher production costs, and a shift in consumer demand toward pork and chicken as beef prices climb.

A few other developments worth a mention. Spain's vertically integrated pork producer COPESE marked its 50th anniversary with 2025 revenue up 14 percent to 150.4 million euros and improving margins.
In Brazil, Embrapa reported rising July production costs for hogs and broilers, while pork exports slipped on weaker Asian demand. A government decree could also bring up to 100 billion reais in farm loans up for renegotiation.
Feed mills in Brazil's hog-heavy Santa Catarina state truck in most of their roughly 8 million tons of annual corn needs. Road delays there sometimes reach 65 to 80 percent of shipments.
Brazil released a new guide aimed at improving on-farm pig welfare, though full details were not yet available at publication. In the UK, new fair-dealing rules for pig purchase contracts took effect August 13th, requiring written terms for all contracts.
Regulator ASCA can fine violators up to 1 percent of turnover. UK food delivery app spending on pork rose 54.7 percent year over year, far outpacing a 2.2 percent decline in the overall takeaway and delivery market.
Denmark's new government proposed livestock reforms, including a 2030 carbon tax on emissions and a phase-out of tail docking. That could tighten European pork supply, since Denmark supplies about 21 percent of UK pork imports.
Brazil's ABPA projects 2026 pork production up about 5 percent to 5.87 million tons, with exports rising to roughly 1.6 million tons. That would cement its position as the world's third-largest pork exporter.
