Tuesday, September 1st. Here is your pork industry news. Today we cover CME cash markets and a bearish U.S. slaughter outlook from Rabobank. We'll also look at a growing tariff standoff between the United States and Canada, feed cost updates from Brazil to France, and European market data.

Let's start with U.S. prices and supply. On August 31st at the CME, October lean hog futures closed up 1 dollar 77 cents at 83 dollars 67 cents. December futures rose 1 dollar 85 cents to 74 dollars 15 cents. Cash hogs were softer that day. National direct negotiated barrows and gilts fell 84 cents. The base price range was 86 to 89 dollars, with a weighted average of 88 dollars 73 cents. Midwest cash hogs traded at 64 dollars. The pork cutout rose 1 dollar 61 cents to 97 dollars 67 cents. Estimated hog slaughter came in at 476,000 head, up 7,000 from a year earlier.

USDA's wholesale pork carcass price was reported separately at 95 dollars 16 cents per hundredweight, up 1 dollar 36 cents, according to The Pig Site. That same day, CME October lean hog futures were quoted up 1.275 cents at 81.9 cents per pound. President Trump is reportedly preparing an executive order aimed at letting producers process their own meat, addressing what he calls a packing monopoly. Agriculture Secretary Rollins said she would announce expanded interstate meat sales starting Monday. She also promised additional support for small processors. Separately, the administration issued a proclamation temporarily expanding low-tariff imports of lean beef trimmings.

Looking further ahead, Rabobank forecasts that U.S. hog slaughter will keep running below year-ago levels through the second half of 2026. The pace of decline should ease as the year goes on. July's weekly slaughter was down 3 percent from a year earlier. That reflects past disease-related culling and seasonal plant maintenance. Rabobank says the second quarter saw ample market-ready hog supplies and disappointing pork demand, which pressured prices. The market began stabilizing in the third quarter. Producers are holding sow numbers steady while raising market weights to offset the lower head count. Disease risk and export uncertainty are keeping expansion plans cautious.

Turning to trade and tariffs. A standoff between the United States and Canada is threatening the flow of more than 3 million Canadian feeder pigs into the U.S. each year. Talks under the USMCA stalled on August 21st and 22nd. The U.S. then imposed 50 percent tariffs on 20 billion dollars of Canadian goods under Section 338 of the Trade Act. Canada has announced dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs set to take effect September 8th. Manitoba alone ships more than 3 million weaned pigs to the U.S. each year. About 2 million of those are finished and slaughtered in Iowa. Industry sources warn that if that flow stops, U.S. finishing farms and packing plants could sit idle. Meanwhile, Canadian farrowing barns could become overcrowded.

In Brazil, the Ministry of Agriculture announced newly approved market access to Costa Rica, Malaysia, and Nigeria. The specific products approved for each destination were not detailed in the announcement. In related trade news, a July 16th ruling exempted fresh Brazilian tilapia fillets from the 25 percent U.S. additional tariff. Brazilian chicken exports to the EU rose 77 percent, as shippers moved early ahead of anticipated EU barriers.

On the feed cost front, Brazilian soybean prices at the port hit their highest level in three years this August. Chinese export demand and tight holding by domestic crushers drove the increase. In the week of August 24th, the CEPEA-ESALQ price benchmarks for the ports of Paranagua and Parana posted their highest daily readings since April 2023. Growers are holding back sales on concerns about El Nino and adverse Northern Hemisphere weather affecting the 2026-27 crop. Buyers are accepting longer payment terms in exchange for locking in supply at these higher prices.

There was better news out of São Paulo state, where the 2025-26 corn crop reached 20.6 million tons. That's up 41 percent from a year earlier, thanks to expanded planted area and higher yields. That extra supply should help push down feed costs for the state's hog and poultry producers.

In Europe, France announced compensation worth several hundred million euros for farmers hit by record heat and drought. 65 percent of the country experienced drought at a once-in-25-years severity. 60 percent of groundwater levels were below normal. July's average temperature of 24.9 degrees Celsius was 3.8 degrees above normal. That surpassed the previous record of 24.3 degrees, set in 2003. Grain corn yields are expected to fall 34 percent, and silage corn 30 percent. As of August 20th, pasture growth was running 36 percent below the 1989-2018 average. More than half of grassland in most regions is in serious deterioration.

In European market news, German finisher hog prices recovered during August, climbing from 1.40 euros to 1.60 euros per kilogram. Extreme heat had slowed daily weight gain, tightening market weights and supply. That coincided with post-vacation consumer demand and processing needs picking back up. 25-kilogram feeder pig prices rose from 30 euros to 41 euros, and sow prices climbed to 0.73 euros. Meanwhile, packer Tönnies has canceled its existing supply agreements and proposed new supply and purchase contracts. Producer groups ISN and the German Farmers' Association are advising producers not to sign for now.

New Eurostat data analyzed by AHDB shows the EU's pig herd fell 0.5 percent in 2025, to 131.5 million head. That's down 10 percent from 2020. Spain's herd fell 3 percent, or about 919,600 head, to 33.6 million. The Netherlands fell 7 percent to 9.5 million. Germany's herd grew 1 percent to 21.5 million, Denmark grew 6 percent to 12.3 million, and Poland grew 2 percent to 9.2 million. The EU's breeding herd rose 0.8 percent to 10.3 million. Piglets under 20 kilograms rose 6.1 percent, to a ten-year high of 42.4 million. AHDB expects European pork production to grow 0.3 percent in 2026.

Spain's own pork production rose 3.2 percent in the first half of 2026, to 2.76 million tons. Slaughter volume rose 2.1 percent to 28.4 million head. Average carcass weight rose from about 96.0 to 97.1 kilograms. That's why production growth outpaced the increase in head count.

Danish Crown plans to send raw material equivalent to about 500,000 hogs a year from its cooperative members to its Polish subsidiary, Sokołów. The company's eventual goal is to double that volume. The material sent will mainly be front legs and hams, while higher-value cuts stay in Denmark. Sokołów's processing ratio already exceeds 70 percent, the highest in the group. Shifting internal raw material there, instead of buying externally, is meant to boost profitability.

Turning to animal health and technology, a veterinary diagnostic laboratory in Illinois has joined the Swine Disease Reporting System network. The state's herd includes 600,000 breeding hogs and 4.65 million market hogs. Officials say the addition underscores the importance of robust animal health surveillance.

Researchers at Chulalongkorn University's veterinary school in Thailand have developed a synthetic boar pheromone. They used gas chromatography to identify 23 key compounds from mature boar scent. In trials, contact with a live boar induced estrus behavior in about 75 percent of gilts. The synthetic scent achieved about 50 percent, compared with 18 percent for an unstimulated control group. Because there is no vaccine or treatment for African swine fever, reducing direct boar contact lowers transmission risk. The findings were published in an international journal in early 2026, and a provisional patent has been filed.

On the policy front, USDA estimates wild pigs are present in 35 states, numbering about 7 million as of 2016. They cause more than 2.5 to 3.4 billion dollars in annual property damage, including over 800 million dollars in crop damage. The 2018 Farm Bill created a 75 million dollar joint feral swine control program. A budget law passed in July 2025 added another 105 million dollars to it. Applications for the first 35 million dollars of that new funding are being accepted through September 21st, 2026. A House-passed farm bill would add another 150 million dollars and extend the program through 2031. Analysis from the University of Tennessee found that in counties running the program, crop insurance claims for corn damage fell. The average claim size dropped from 70 acres to just 10 acres.

That's your pork industry news for today.
