Saturday, August 22nd. Here is your pork industry news.

Coming up, African swine fever keeps spreading across Europe with new outbreaks in Hungary, Poland, and Serbia. CME lean hog futures swung from a slide to a bounce over two trading sessions. And two major packer deals are on the table, including JBS moving to fully absorb Pilgrim's Pride and Pilgrim's Europe expanding into premium UK pork.

Let's start with prices and the futures market, where the past two trading sessions told two different stories. On Wednesday, August 20th, the CME October lean hog contract fell one and a quarter cents to close at 80.225 cents. The USDA pork carcass cutout dropped as well, down one dollar and nine cents to 96 dollars and 49 cents. That same day, officials in Mexico's Sonora state confirmed the state's first case of New World screwworm near the US border. Emergency surveillance and containment measures were activated in response.

The next day, Thursday the 21st, lean hogs bounced back on what traders described as oversold conditions. The October contract rose 65 points to 80.87 cents, while December gained 77 points to close at 71.25 cents. On the cash side, the National Direct cash hog weighted average climbed one dollar 93 cents to 94 dollars 27 cents. The pork cutout rose one dollar 47 cents to 97 dollars 96 cents. Estimated slaughter for the week came in at 454,000 head, up 34,000 from the prior week.

Now to disease and biosecurity, where African swine fever continues to expand its footprint across Europe. New culling operations and expanded control zones have been set up in several countries this week. In Hungary's Baranya county, the food safety authority Nebih confirmed the virus on three farms near the Croatian border. Two outbreaks were found on August 12th, at Dravafok with 34 pigs and Okorag with 19 pigs, and a third followed on August 18th at Kolked with 27 pigs. In total, about 80 animals were culled, and authorities set up a three kilometer protection zone and a ten kilometer surveillance zone around the sites.

In Poland, a commercial farm in the Kuyavian Pomeranian region with more than 3,000 pigs became the country's third commercial ASF case this year, confirmed on July 22nd. The farm sits about two kilometers from a second case found on July 9th, which involved 774 pigs. Protection and surveillance zones now cover 51 farms and roughly 42,000 animals. Across Europe in July, domestic pig ASF cases totaled 186 in seven countries, up from 164 in June.

Serbia has been especially hard hit. In July, the government declared a state of emergency in nine municipalities as African swine fever kept spreading. The outbreak now covers seven districts, 12 municipalities, and 58 settlements, affecting 734 farms in total. Around 30,000 pigs have died or been culled since the summer outbreak began. Serbia recorded 23 of Europe's July cases, second only to Croatia.

The spread in Spain and the wider disruption it's causing were also on the agenda when the UK's Ulster Farmers' Union and Ireland's IFA met in Dublin this week. The two groups discussed African swine fever's impact on European markets. They also addressed the hardship facing Northern Ireland producers after Sofina Foods ended its supply contract at the Cookstown plant. They agreed that keeping contaminated pork products out of the island of Ireland and strengthening border checks remain the top priorities.

Separately, foot and mouth disease serotype A resurfaced in Vietnam for the first time in eight years. Four outbreaks were confirmed in June across three northern provinces, including Cao Bang, infecting 58 animals in total, made up of 46 buffalo, 11 goats, and one pig. Because the existing serotype O vaccine doesn't protect against serotype A, authorities carried out ring vaccination with an A-specific vaccine. No new cases have been reported since July 12th. Vietnam's 2026 to 2030 national plan targets a 30 percent reduction in outbreaks and forced culling.

Turning now to packer and corporate developments, where two acquisition stories stood out this week. Pilgrim's Europe announced on August 20th that it has agreed to acquire Walkers Deli and Sausage from Samworth Brothers. The deal still needs approval from the UK's Competition and Markets Authority and the completion of employee consultations. Walkers was founded in 1824 and employs about 1,150 people in Leicester, and the acquisition strengthens Pilgrim's position in the UK's premium, value added pork category.

In the US, JBS already owns 82 percent of Pilgrim's Pride. On August 21st, the company proposed acquiring the remaining shares through a stock exchange valued at roughly one point two billion dollars. If completed, the deal would make Pilgrim's Pride a wholly owned subsidiary of JBS.

Also on August 21st, Hormel Foods petitioned for certification of judgment in the ongoing pork price fixing litigation. Plaintiffs in the case include roughly 100 direct action parties, spread across eight federal circuits and 11 federal districts, who have filed about 40 lawsuits in total.

Now for trade and tariffs. As of August 21st, the United States gained expanded market access to Nigeria, Africa's third largest economy. US beef cuts and nearly all US pork cuts, with the exception of bone in ham, can now be exported there.

On the feed and grain side tied to trade, China's soybean imports from the United States surged in July, according to Suinocultura Industrial. Chinese ports received one point one one million tonnes of US soybeans, up 164 percent from a year earlier. Brazilian soybean arrivals fell 5.9 percent to nine point seven eight million tonnes in July. Even so, Brazil still leads for the year to date, having shipped 44.53 million tonnes from January through July, up five point three seven percent from last year. China has reaffirmed its commitment to purchase 25 million tonnes of US soybeans annually through 2028.

Feed costs are also in focus after the Pro Farmer Crop Tour wrapped up on August 20th. The tour's Iowa corn yield estimate came in at 193.98 bushels per acre, down from 198.43 on last year's tour. Minnesota's estimate was 199.01 bushels per acre, down from 202.86 a year ago. Both states came in below last year's tour figures but above their three year averages. USDA's August 12th forecast still points to the second highest corn production on record, just short of the record 2025 crop.

On policy and regulation, the European Union's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation took effect across the bloc on August 12th. It restricts food contact packaging containing PFAS compounds above set limits from entering the EU market. The rule replaces a patchwork of national requirements with a single EU wide framework covering the entire packaging lifecycle. It supports the bloc's push toward a circular economy and its 2050 climate neutrality goal.

In Washington, an August 21st policy update noted that farm bill deliberations are expected to resume when Congress returns in September. Trade policy and new tools to fight New World screwworm are also drawing close attention from the pork industry. The update characterized hog operation margins as holding up well for now.

Finally, a look at conditions in individual country markets. In Brazil, live hog prices have fallen to levels near the lowest on record since Cepea began tracking the market, according to reporting on August 21st. Oversupply and weak demand have pushed many producers into chronic losses, and some are exiting the business altogether.

And in Japan, NHK reported on August 21st that retail prices for pork and chicken have hit record highs, with the impact being felt in Ehime prefecture as well. Further details on specific price levels were not immediately available.

That covers the futures and cash markets, the disease outbreaks, the corporate deals, and the trade and policy developments shaping the pork industry today. That's your pork industry news for today.
