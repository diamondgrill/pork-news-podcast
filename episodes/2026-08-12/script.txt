Wednesday, August 12th. Here is your pork industry news. Today we're covering a leadership shakeup and a major joint venture at JBS. We'll also look at swinging hog prices in the U.S. and Brazil. Then we turn to African swine fever developments in Spain and Italy, plus a busy day for pork trade data.

We start with packers and corporate news. Brazilian meat giant JBS announced a global CEO transition effective January 2027. Wesley Batista Filho, currently head of JBS USA, will become global CEO. Current CEO Gilberto Tomazoni is stepping down after fourteen years in the role, eight of them as global chief executive. He will move to vice chairman and senior adviser, while remaining chairman of Pilgrim's Pride. Separately, JBS agreed to a joint venture with Indonesia's sovereign wealth fund, Danantara, to invest in protein production. The venture will target Indonesia, Southeast Asia, Australia, and New Zealand. The fund will contribute two and a half billion dollars, with eight hundred million dollars paid at closing and the rest paid in over as long as three years. JBS will contribute its entire Australia and New Zealand operations to the venture as an in kind investment. The joint venture also plans to raise up to another two and a half billion dollars in debt financing. Both partners are eyeing an eventual public listing after a five year lockup period. In Canada, Canada Packers reported second quarter revenue down eight point eight percent year over year, according to The Pig Site. Falling pork prices, a weaker yen, and the loss of a ham boning contract were cited as the main reasons for the decline. And in Finland, HKFoods reported profit up nineteen percent, driven by growth in retail and food service sales. Most of its full year 2026 profit is expected in the second half.

Turning to prices and markets. Brownfield Ag News reported that Tuesday's CME close saw the October lean hog contract settle at eighty three dollars and sixty seven cents per hundredweight. That was up one dollar and forty five cents on the day. Remember, the CME lean hog contract is a live hog futures contract, named for the hog breed rather than for lean meat. Corn and soybean meal futures were mixed in that same session. Earlier in the week, The Pig Site reported the U.S. pork cutout recovered to one hundred one dollars and forty five cents per hundredweight on Friday, August 7th. That was up two dollars and nineteen cents from the day before. Lean hog futures were mixed that day, with the August contract flat and the October contract edging higher. But by the end of last week, the October lean hog contract had actually fallen to a four week low of eighty three dollars and two and a half cents. The CME Lean Hog Index also slipped, down to ninety seven dollars and seventeen cents. Weekly U.S. pork export sales came in at twenty seven thousand three hundred tons, down twenty three percent from the prior week. That figure was still up six percent from the four week average, with Mexico and Japan the top destinations. On the global front, the FAO Food Price Index rose to one hundred thirty one point one in July, its highest level since January of 2023. Cereal prices, led by a five point eight percent jump in wheat, drove the increase. Meanwhile the meat index fell two point eight percent from June's record high, with pork, poultry, and beef all easing. In Brazil, live hog prices in Sao Paulo state hit their third lowest level since records began, according to Cepea data cited by Suinocultura Industrial. Prices there have fallen forty three percent since December of last year, as weak domestic demand fails to keep pace with supply. Wholesale pork prices in greater Sao Paulo told a similar story, falling one point nine percent in July to eight reais and forty one centavos per kilogram. Supply from southern Brazil outpaced demand there as well.

Now to disease and biosecurity. In Spain, the African swine fever outbreak in Catalonia's wild boar population continues to grow. Pig333 reported one new case in Barcelona city and neighboring Sant Just Desvern. That brings cumulative positive wild boar to three hundred seventy five across thirteen municipalities. More than ten thousand three hundred animals have tested negative so far. Over ten thousand wild boar have also been culled within the infection zone. In Italy, African swine fever has reached the native Cinta Senese breed in Tuscany. Suinocultura Industrial reported the region's first case was confirmed in late June in Massa-Carrara province. That led to the culling of three hundred Cinta Senese pigs, roughly ten percent of the breed's estimated worldwide population of three thousand. Breeders are now considering moving genetic stock to isolated islands to protect the breed, since its free range husbandry raises the risk of contact with wild boar. In the U.S., The Pig Site reported that a PRRS virus variant identified in Iowa in 2023 has spread rapidly. It now accounts for more than thirty eight percent of PRRS sequences detected in the state within about a year. Its parent lineage has driven more than forty five percent of all U.S. detections since it emerged in 2020. Researchers at Iowa State identified one thousand two hundred fourteen matching sequences between January of 2023 and March of 2025. PRRS is estimated to cost the U.S. swine industry more than one billion dollars annually. Meanwhile, USDA announced an additional thirty five million dollars for feral swine damage management. Applications are open across fourteen states, including Texas and Florida, with a deadline of September 21st.

On trade, USMEF data cited by Euromeat News showed U.S. pork exports to Japan rose seventeen percent in the first half of the year. Volume reached one hundred eighty nine thousand seven hundred tons, worth seven hundred twenty one million dollars. Overall U.S. pork exports in June fell six percent year over year, as Mexico and Colombia had temporarily halted variety meat imports over Aujeszky's disease. That restriction was lifted by July, but it cut variety meat exports twenty one percent for the month. First half exports overall were still up four percent by volume and three percent by value. Separately, The Pig Site reported U.S. pork exports to the Philippines jumped seventy four percent in May, as African swine fever continues to curb Philippine domestic production. In turn, the Philippines' own pork imports rose eleven percent in the first half of the year, with Brazil as the top supplier. Brazil's own pork exports also grew. ABPA reported July shipments of one hundred thirty one thousand five hundred tons, up three point seven percent from a year earlier. That volume was worth two hundred ninety eight million dollars. And Brazil's government is accelerating trade talks with the European Union. At stake is one point eight billion dollars in annual meat exports, threatened by tightening EU environmental traceability and sanitary facility rules. Brazil's agriculture ministry is also pursuing new market access, announcing openings to Malaysia and Cuba in parallel.

In other developments today, France's pork production value is expected to fall nine point four percent in 2025 on lower prices, even as output volumes hold steady. The UK's Standard Pig Price posted its biggest weekly rise in five years for the week ending August 2nd, according to Pig World. Spain's live pig imports fell thirty five point six percent in the first half, as trade restrictions followed a wild boar African swine fever case last November. About eighty percent of those imports were piglets, and the top supplier, the Netherlands, saw its shipments drop nearly twenty nine percent. Pig Progress published a report on how Denmark rapidly brought a PRRS outbreak under control. The FDA granted emergency authorization for a wound spray called CLiK Extra to prevent New World screwworm in cattle, pigs, goats, and sheep. AHDB released its second quarter production cost report for UK pork producers. In South Korea, six pork distributors were indicted on price cartel allegations involving roughly one point two trillion won, though full details remain unconfirmed. The UK's Food Standards Agency opened a criminal investigation after seizing four tons of illegal meat from the market. Spain approved a new certification protocol for Iberico pork, which takes effect in April of 2027 after a nine month transition. And U.S. health officials submitted the federal government's first proposed definition of ultra-processed foods for final review. They also proposed a new rule requiring FDA notification for food additive safety determinations.

That's your pork industry news for today.
