Friday, August 28th. Here is your pork industry news.

Today we're covering renewed warnings about a squeeze on European producers. We'll also look at a shift in the balance of global pork trade. And we'll run through mixed signals from the American cash and futures markets.

Let's start with prices and the pressure building on producers in several regions. In the United Kingdom, the National Pig Association warned on August 27th that the industry is teetering on the brink. The NPA pointed to Europe-wide oversupply and rock-bottom hog prices as the core of the problem. It also cited an unprecedented wave of contract terminations by processors. Independent producers are being forced to sell below their cost of production. Some are losing thousands of pounds a week as a result. Sow herd reductions and farm exits are accelerating because of the pressure. The NPA is now pushing for a government support package. It is also calling for an industry roundtable and greater engagement with the Agricultural Supply Chain Adjudicator.

The UK's cash market reflected some of that strain this week. For the week ending August 22nd, the GB SPP price fell one point one two pence to 178.39 pence per kilogram. That is roughly 30 pence below where prices stood a year ago. Estimated slaughter for the week came in at 166,066 head, up 2,000 from the previous week. Average carcass weight rose as well, gaining 0.73 kilograms to reach 90.75 kilograms. According to Defra, July's UK clean pig slaughter totaled 909,000 head. That was up five point eight percent from a year earlier. Adding to cost pressure on producers, London's feed wheat contract for November rose five pounds to 210 pounds per tonne.

Producers in South America are contending with their own price pressures this week. Brazil is seeing similarly weak prices on its side of the Atlantic. According to Cepea, the average live hog price in the Sao Paulo SP-5 region was five point one eight reais per kilo in July. That is the third-lowest price on record for that particular region. It is even below the worst point of the 2022 crisis. Back then, the inflation-adjusted price bottomed out at five point nine seven reais in February. Based on preliminary IBGE data, production is estimated to peak between July and September this year. That seasonal oversupply could limit the usual fourth-quarter price rebound. Both producers and packers may see their margins squeezed as a result.

In Japan, a headline-only report from TBS News Dig said retail pork prices have climbed. The report cites an increase of 300 yen per kilogram. It links the rise to a halt in imports of Spanish pork. The restaurant chain Saizeriya has suspended sales of prosciutto as a result of the disruption. The full article text was not available at the time of this report, so further details remain unconfirmed.

Turning now to the American futures and cash markets, CME Lean Hog futures were mixed on August 27th. Remember, Lean Hog contracts track live hogs, named for the lean hog breed rather than for cuts of pork meat. The October live hog contract slipped 27 cents to 80 dollars and 62 cents. Meanwhile the December contract gained 22 cents to reach 71 dollars and 20 cents. On the cash side, the National Daily Direct weighted average for barrows and gilts fell 99 cents to 89 dollars and 76 cents. The pork cutout dropped as well, losing one dollar and 75 cents to settle at 93 dollars and 80 cents. Loin, butt, picnic, rib, and belly primals all traded lower on the day. Estimated slaughter for the day was 486,000 head. That was up 44,000 from the previous week, though still down 1,000 from a year ago.

Now to trade and exports. According to USMEF's analysis of USDA data, U.S. pork exports in June totaled 224,822 tons. That figure is down six percent from a year earlier. Export value fell nine percent over the same period, to 624.5 million dollars. Mexico and Colombia had halted imports of U.S. pork variety meat starting in early May. The halt was tied to concerns over pseudorabies, and it cut variety meat exports by 21 percent. Those restrictions were fully lifted during the month of July. Looking at the first half of the year, cumulative exports reached one point five one million tons. That is up four percent from a year ago. Export value for the same period rose three percent, reaching four point two two billion dollars. Shipments to Japan, Central America, and the Dominican Republic are all running at a record pace.

Rabobank's latest World Pork Map highlighted a broader shift in global trade patterns. China's import share surged during the African swine fever crisis between 2018 and 2021. That share has since fallen sharply. The report says global pork trade has now entered a new phase. Import demand is spreading across a wider range of countries rather than concentrating in China. Excluding variety meat, Mexico is now the world's largest pork importer. The Philippines and Vietnam are also growing their share of Southeast Asian demand. On the export side, the European Union remains the top supplier for now. But environmental regulation and rising costs are constraining the EU's growth. Rabobank expects the United States to close that gap over time. It could eventually take the lead in global pork exports.

Next, a look at herd numbers and disease status around the world. In the Philippines, the hog inventory stood at eight point nine three million head as of the end of June. That is according to Pig333. The figure is down one percent from a year earlier, a relatively modest decline.

In Colombia, the picture looks quite different. Figures from the Porkcolombia-FNP classical swine fever eradication program show strong growth. The national hog inventory rose nine point three percent from last year, reaching 12,933,869 head. Large-scale technified operations account for 61.7 percent of that total. Within the commercial industrial tier, the breeding sow count grew the fastest of all. It rose 46.5 percent to reach 109,147 head. The number of registered hog farms also grew, reaching 218,015. That is up 12.7 percent year over year.

In Chile, a headline-only report from 3tres3 said apparent pork consumption rose 10 percent year over year. The increase covers the first half of 2026. The full text of the report was not available, so absolute consumption figures remain unconfirmed.

On animal health, Spanish authorities in Catalonia reported some encouraging news. There was a second consecutive week with zero new African swine fever positives among wild boar. That covers the period following the August 20th update. The cumulative total remains at 67 outbreaks across 13 municipalities. That breaks down to three primary cases and 64 secondary cases. The total of positive wild boar stands at 379, unchanged from before. Testing has produced 11,197 negative results to date. Cumulative captures within the infected zone have reached 11,011 animals. Trapping and carcass searches are continuing across the region. All existing biosecurity measures remain fully in place.

A brief update now on the EU herd. AHDB published its latest EU pork update on August 27th. The headline indicates that the pace of EU herd contraction is easing. It also suggests that breeding sow numbers have stabilized. The full article was not available, so further detail could not be confirmed at this time.

Finally, a packer development from the United States. Hormel Foods reported its third-quarter fiscal 2026 results on August 27th. Those results reflected the impact of the company's ongoing portfolio-shaping actions. Citing a solid quarter and strong performance so far this year, Hormel raised its full-year adjusted profit outlook. The company also narrowed the range of that guidance.

That wraps up today's coverage of prices, trade, herd figures, animal health, and company results across the pork industry. That's your pork industry news for today.
