Thursday, September 3rd. Here is your pork industry news.

Today we start with the European Union suspending Brazilian meat imports, a Japanese report on shifting pork suppliers, and a look at hog and grain futures. We will also cover disease surveillance progress in Denmark and Canada, plus several production and trade updates from Europe and Brazil.

Let's begin with trade and tariffs. The European Commission announced on September 1st that it will suspend imports of Brazilian meat starting September 3rd. According to The Pig Site, the move comes because Brazil could not guarantee compliance with EU rules banning the use of antimicrobials for growth promotion. The EU had already signaled back in May that Brazil could be removed from its approved supplier list. Brazil was the European Union's second largest non-EU beef supplier in 2025, and the EU requires guarantees covering an animal's entire life. Audits on poultry and honey are expected to wrap up this weekend, though no timeline has been given for lifting the meat suspension.

In Japan, a headline-only report from Nikkei, picked up via Google News, says Brazilian pork jumped about sixty percent year over year in Japan's imports from April through July. That made Brazil the top supplier. The same report says Spanish pork imports have stopped. Because only the headline was captured, details on import volumes, values, and the reasons behind the halt in Spanish shipments are not yet confirmed.

Speaking of Spain, Pig333 reports that the Philippines has resumed importing Spanish pork. Spain's agriculture minister, Luis Planas, met with the Philippines' agriculture secretary in Manila. He thanked him for restarting imports from Spain's African swine fever-free regions as of May 22nd this year. The move rests on the principle of regionalization for African swine fever control. The Philippines is Spain's third largest pork export market, accounting for fourteen percent of Spanish pork exports. Spain is the second largest supplier to that market after Brazil. Spain's pork exports to the Philippines were worth about 86.78 million euros in 2025, from 175 certified facilities.

Germany also had trade news this week. Euromeat News reports that German pork and pork product exports rose 1.7 percent year over year in the first half of 2026, to roughly 1.08 million tons. Of that, pork and pork products made up 894,000 tons and edible offal made up 184,000 tons. Shipments within the EU27 fell about 3 percent to 889,000 tons, while exports outside the EU jumped about 30 percent, from 146,000 tons to 189,000 tons. That leaves the EU accounting for about 82 percent of German pork exports. By destination, Italy took 170,000 tons, down 2 percent. The Netherlands took 160,000 tons, up 6 percent. Poland took 76,000 tons, up 18 percent. And shipments to the United Kingdom rose about 45 percent.

Now to prices and futures. The Pig Site reports that the USDA's national pork carcass cutout value came in at 97 dollars and 68 cents per hundredweight on September 1st. That was up just one cent from the day before, essentially flat. The CME's benchmark October lean hog contract settled that day at 83.65 cents per pound, down slightly. Cattle futures moved lower too, after livestock movement restrictions were partially lifted in parts of Texas and New Mexico. October live cattle fell 60 cents to 212.075 cents per pound, and October feeder cattle dropped one dollar and 62 and a half cents to 315.40 cents per pound.

Brownfield Ag News has the next day's closing numbers. On September 2nd, the CME's October lean hog contract closed at 83.77 cents per pound, up about 12 hundredths of a cent from the prior close. Feed grains were mostly softer that day. December corn slipped two and a half cents to 5 dollars and 43 and a half cents a bushel. October soybean meal fell two dollars and 80 cents to 342 dollars and 90 cents a ton. November soybeans dropped seven and a half cents to 13 dollars and ten and a quarter cents a bushel. In cattle, October live cattle closed at 210.17 cents per pound, down one dollar and 90 cents. October feeder cattle closed at 314.37 cents per pound, down one dollar and 2 cents.

Turning to disease and biosecurity. Denmark's PRRS eradication effort is making progress on the island of Lolland, the country's fourth largest island. According to a report dated August 14th and carried by Pig333, all municipalities on the islands of Møn, Lolland, and Falster now have zero PRRS-positive farms. Bornholm, Samsø, and parts of Funen have also been registered as clean regions. On the island of Zealand, only a handful of positive farms remain, all in the Holbæk municipality, and each has a recovery plan in place.

In Canada, a six-year study of wild boar in Alberta is raising biosecurity concerns. Suinocultura Industrial reports that the University of Calgary and the Alberta provincial government tested 331 wild boar captured between 2018 and 2024 for 17 pathogens. Fifty-six percent of the animals carried two or more pathogens considered significant to commercial pig farming at the same time. The bacteria that causes swine erysipelas was found in 100 percent of the animals tested. PCV2 was found in 33 percent, PCV3 in 27 percent, rotavirus A in 23 percent, rotavirus C in 5 percent, parvovirus in 18 percent, PRRS virus in 1 percent, and Eimeria in 40 percent. Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, porcine deltacoronavirus, Seneca Valley virus, and transmissible gastroenteritis virus were not detected in any of the animals. The researchers are calling for better fencing and gates, along with measures to keep wild boar away from feed and water sources on farms.

Finally, some packer and production developments. Euromeat News reports that JBS has earned the Gold Seal in Brazil's GHG Protocol Program for the seventh year in a row. It has held gold status every year since 2019, based on its 2025 greenhouse gas inventory. The company is targeting a 30 percent cut in its scope 1 and scope 2 emissions intensity by 2030, measured against a 2019 baseline. It also aims for a 70 percent cut at its processing facilities by 2050. Its Friboi subsidiary, working with Âmbar Energia, is investing 65 million reais to expand biomethane production at three plants. Those are Campo Grande II in Mato Grosso do Sul, and Lins and Andradina in São Paulo state. The expansion will add more than 14 million cubic meters of new annual output.

In Brazil's Paraná state, Suinocultura Industrial reports that the Capal agricultural cooperative and Aurora Coop are planning a 30 percent expansion of swine production. Capal has launched an integrated production program in response to a capacity expansion at Aurora's processing plant in Castro, Paraná. Under the program, the cooperative supplies piglets, feed, veterinary medicine, and technical support. Producers provide housing and labor, managing up to 1,100 hogs per barn with family labor. In the first half of 2026, the cooperative handled 15,500 tons for slaughter and 2,700 tons of piglets. It currently works with about 50 hog farms around the town of Arapoti.

In Portugal, provisional data cited by GPP from the national statistics institute shows pig slaughter rose 4.2 percent year over year in the first half of 2026. That reached just over 2.1 million head, an increase of 84,202 animals. Total carcass weight rose 4.4 percent to 185,467 tons, and average carcass weight rose from 87.9 to 88.1 kilograms. Piglet slaughter rose 6.8 percent to 571,990 head, and cull breeding pig slaughter rose 4.5 percent to 24,937 head. In total, 2,701,405 animals, up 4.7 percent, and 193,110.8 tons of carcass weight, up 4.4 percent, were judged fit for human consumption.

Germany's own pig sector also grew modestly in the first half of the year. Citing Destatis figures, 3tres3 reports that German pig slaughter rose 0.5 percent year over year, up 107,000 head, to 22.2 million animals. Pork production rose 0.5 percent as well, up 10,300 tons, to 2.2 million tons. Domestically raised animals made up 21.9 million head, or 98.4 percent of the total, up 0.8 percent. Animals of foreign origin fell 14 percent to 349,200 head. Pork accounted for 63.7 percent of Germany's total meat production in the first half of the year, ahead of poultry at 22.3 percent and beef at 13.7 percent.

And in the United States, The Pig Site reports that Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins announced a pilot program on September 1st at the Farm Progress Show in Boone, Iowa. It is aimed at modernizing USDA crop data collection. The pilot will combine satellite imagery, geospatial tools, and crop models with producer surveys. USDA will work with partners including NASA to improve the accuracy of acreage and yield estimates. The effort follows criticism over the reliability of USDA statistics after staff reductions. Grain prices had already fallen more than 5 percent following the release of final estimates in January. The department is also considering the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning in the process.

That's your pork industry news for today.
